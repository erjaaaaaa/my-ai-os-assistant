# Eriks Petersons — Voice & Tone Style Guide

> **Purpose:** reference for drafting messages, emails and posts in Eriks's
> voice. **Loaded only when something is being drafted — never at the start of
> a run.**
>
> **Provenance:** copied on 2026-09-07 from the guide built for Eriks's work
> instance, at Eriks's request, with colleagues' names, work-only registers
> (board reports, team announcements, people management) and the work chat
> section removed. The evidence base below is the original one and is
> unchanged; nothing personal was sampled. Refresh it from personal sent mail
> when the drafts start needing tone edits rather than content edits.
>
> **Evidence base:** ~200 team-chat messages sampled month-by-month across
> Sep 2025 – Aug 2026 (channels, DMs, group DMs, all three languages,
> including one-word replies), 6 long-form reflection posts read in full, and
> ~100 email threads from the sent folder with 15 messages read in full text —
> covering recurring reports, agendas, long directive emails, structured
> feedback, crisis threads, drafted external letters, and short replies in
> English, Russian and Latvian.

Senior leader in payments and fintech. Through mid-2026 a chief executive —

## 2. The seven things that define the voice

1. **Every message has an ask, and the ask is labelled.** *"One ask from
   you"*, *"Two things I'd like from you before we speak"*, *"Pre-work:"*,
   *"Please provide a clear resolution plan with your actions on each by end
   of this week."* Information without a request is rare.
2. **Why before what.** Context first, then mechanics. Opens with a literal
   question as a heading — *"Why is the non-standard approach important
   here?"* — answers it in two lines, then proposes.
3. **Bad news stated flatly.** *"Margin has dropped drastically this week."*
   *"Unfortunately, don't have enough context to provide a relevant update
   here."* *"Please kill it. It's not a good deal."* No cushioning, no spin,
   no burying.
4. **Proposes, then asks.** Rarely decrees. Puts a concrete option on the
   table with numbers attached, then hands it back: *"I'd suggest reducing it
   by 70-80% at least (exact math / analysis to be confirmed). What do you
   think?"*
5. **Bias to shipping over perfecting.** *"Let's roll it out and improve as we
   go."* *"A bit of long shot, but let's go allin on this, nothing to lose."*
   *"First steps are the most difficult ones."*
6. **Delegation by naming, with the reason attached.** *"@[name] adding you as
   a voice to say what goes / not goes well."* *"@[name] adding you to advise
   on technical limitations we might have."*
7. **Openly human in public.** Health, family, failures, sleep stats, missed
   weeks, own typos. This is deliberate and it is a large part of why people
   respond.

## 3. Email — structure

### Openings by register
| Situation | Opening |
|---|---|
| Group, warm | `Hi team,` / `Hey team,` |
| Whole group, neutral | `Hi everyone,` / `Team,` |
| Formal / directive | `Dear All,` |
| Recurring report | `Good afternoon,` / `Good evening,` / `Good morning,` (time-accurate) |
| Individual, neutral | `Hi [Name],` |
| Individual, warm | `Hey [Name],` / `Hi mate,` / `Hey mate,` |
| Russian | `Привет!` / `[Имя], привет!` / diminutive vocative |
| Latvian | `Sveiks` / `Sveika!` / `Čau` |

Never "I hope this email finds you well" or equivalent. The first line after
the greeting is the point.

### Sign-offs
- `Best,` + `Erik` — default, most common
- `Best regards,` + `Erik` — wider distribution / more formal
- `Erik` alone — short replies, and most Russian emails
- `Ēriks` — Latvian

### Agenda / process emails
- Exact time ranges: `09:15 - 09:45 - [item]`
- ` → ` links item to result, then bolded `*Output:* `
- `#####################` as a divider
- Dates as `DD.MM.YYYY` or `DD-DD.MM` (`by 15.07.2026`, `agenda (01-03.07)`)
- Named owner on every line
- Closes by inviting gaps: *"If there are any suggestions on something I have
  not covered - please let me know."*

### Recap emails
Decisions as a flat numbered list, each with `- owner: [name]` and
`- by DD.MM.YYYY`, then *"If there's anything that I've missed, feel free to
add."*

### Closers actually used (pick from these; don't invent new ones)
- "As always, any questions or comments - don't hesitate."
- "Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate to reach out directly or in this thread."
- "If there are any suggestions on something I have not covered - please let me know."
- "If there's anything that I've missed, feel free to add."
- "Obviously, feel free to chip in if you have any ideas here."
- "If I'm missing anything, please add."
- "Let me know." / "Please LMK."
- "What do you think?"

## 4. Sentence-level mechanics

- **Dash: spaced hyphen ` - `, never an em dash.** The single most consistent
  fingerprint, and it holds across all three languages. *"June closed €24.8m -
  €4m above target"*, *"это не вопрос способностей - скорее вопрос масштаба
  фокуса"*, *"Ja ir lielāks skočs - labāk"*.
- **Slashes for paired concepts:** `modify / add`, `compliance / reporting`,
  `volume/revenue`, `gold/silver/bronze`, `committed vs delivered`, `what goes
  / not goes well`, `Goal / Type / Deadline`.
- **`vs`** for comparisons, lowercase, no period.
- **Emoticons over emoji in prose:** `:)` and `;)`. Emoji codes in chat only.
- **Questions stack, one per line**, short, no preamble.
- **Subject-dropping when reporting a gap:** *"Unfortunately, cannot provide
  the breakdown"*, *"don't have enough context"*, *"Realised I've missed the
  week 17 update"*.
- **Contractions throughout.** Never stiff.
- **Bold mid-sentence** on the thing that must not be missed: *"be careful to
  populate the correct one for Monday."*
- **Softeners before firm asks:** "slightly", "a bit", "I guess", "perhaps",
  "I'd propose", "I would love to". *"Could you please redo it slightly"*
  precedes a complete respec.
- **`imo`** lowercase. **`100%`** as agreement. **`Fair enough.`** when
  conceding.
- **Mild swearing with close people only.** Never to externals, never in
  anything formal.
- Paragraphs 1–3 sentences. Longer becomes bullets.

### Vocabulary that is his
`the drill`, `chip in`, `wrap up`, `let's roll it out`, `go allin`, `by idea`,
`a sort of`, `pretty much`, `obviously`, `as I understand`, `fair enough`,
`timeboxed`, `lock the baseline`, `bottlenecks`, `slippage`, `run-rate`,
`capacity`, `mate`, `bud`, `Grand!`, `real champ`, `no pressure`, `had no
doubts`, `Over and out.`

### Vocabulary that is not his — avoid
Em dashes. "I hope this finds you well." "Circling back." "Touch base."
"Synergy." "Leverage" as a verb. "Deep dive" as a noun. "Per my last email."
"Kindly." Exclamation-mark enthusiasm. Three-sentence apologies. "I just
wanted to check whether perhaps…" Corporate abstraction generally — he says
*"putting AI to work where it actually brings value"*, not *"leveraging AI to
drive value creation."*

## 5. Chat and short messages

- **DMs** — rapid-fire fragments, one thought per message, seconds apart,
  lowercase starts, often no terminal punctuation. **Do not consolidate a DM
  into one tidy paragraph** — that reads wrong. Frequently opens with just the
  name or a greeting as its own message. Thanks are immediate and unadorned.
  Sends a bare `1.` or `2.` as a message to number a list being built live.
- **Short replies** — one to two lines, warm, forward-moving: *"Looks great!
  First steps are the most difficult ones. Let's roll it out and improve as we
  go."* / *"well done, had no doubts"* / *"no pressure"*.
- **Absence note** — three lines: not feeling great, switching off, call if
  urgent.
- **Social posts** — three short lines with emoji: a greeting, a wish, a close.

## 6. Language switching

| Language | Where | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **English** | Anything with a non-Russian speaker on it, external comms, structured writing | Fluent, functional, structured. Occasional non-native rhythm — part of the voice |
| **Russian** | 1:1 and small-group messages and email with Russian speakers | Informal, lowercase, clipped, fast, unproofread. Also used for long-form structured feedback |
| **Latvian** | 1:1 with Latvian speakers | Very short, functional, affectionate diminutives |

Code-switches mid-message when natural: *"Привет! I'm back."* Don't force it,
don't sanitise it.

### Russian specifics
- Vocative diminutives constantly (`Наташ`, `Кать`, `Саша`, `Костя`)
- Signs `Эрик`
- Same ` - ` dash, same `:)` / `;)`
- Anglicisms in Cyrillic: `таргеты`, `роудмапа`, `оунит`, `лидить`, `мит`,
  `комплаенс`, `нотификации`, `апрувит`, `заматать`, `скочем`
- Latin-script terms left inline: `Goal / Type / Deadline`, `gross profit`
- **Types fast and does not proofread Russian.** Recurring: `сдесь`, `что-бы`,
  `так-же`, `получаетьcя`, `помошь`. Reproduce the *speed and warmth*; don't
  manufacture errors, but don't over-polish either — hyper-correct Russian
  reads as someone else.
- Casual/slangy in DMs: `Йо`, `ладно`, `ну гуд`, `Вел дан! ;)`, `Филигранная
  работа`

### Latvian specifics
- Opens `Sveiks` / `Sveika!` / `Čau`, signs `Ēriks`
- Diminutive vocatives
- Extremely short: `Oktobri`, `Jā, jo tas sāks darboties septembrī`, `Kad
  būs q4 forecast, varēs saprast`
- English nouns inline: `q4 forecast`
- Often drops diacritics when typing fast
- Warm brevity even on bad news: *"Paldies par feedback. Žēl, bet nu ko
  darīt... Saprotu. Keep in touch ;)"*

## 7. External / formal register

When drafting for a company, a landlord, a clinic, a school or any
counterparty, the register shifts up considerably — full sentences, no
contractions in the formal parts, no dashes-as-asides, formal courtesy, and a
cooperative close even when the content is hard. The shape: state the
position without hedging → acknowledge the constraint → offer cooperation →
attach a concrete ask. Even at maximum formality the ask is still there.

Persuasive external email keeps the structure but softens the close: *"I'd
like to elaborate further in case I didn't manage to explain myself well.
[…] I hope this clarifies the proposed arrangement."*

Warm/brief external, when the relationship is easy: *"Grand! Thanks, mate!"*

## 8. Length calibration

| Type | Target |
|---|---|
| DM reply | 1–12 words, often several consecutive messages |
| Chat reply | 1–2 sentences |
| Short email reply | 2–4 lines + sign-off |
| Approval | 1–2 lines. "Approved, considering 2.5k means minimum 2 persons. Best, Erik" |
| Alignment / proposal email | 150–250 words, bulleted, named owners |
| Longer directive or plan | 300–500 words, numbered sections, bolded deadlines |
| Report / agenda | As long as the structure needs; density over brevity |

---

## 9. Quick self-check before handing over a draft

- [ ] Is the ask explicit — who, what, by when?
- [ ] Any em dash? Replace with ` - `.
- [ ] Corporate filler in the first line? Cut it.
- [ ] Bad news softened? Unsoften it.
- [ ] If it's a firm correction, is there a softener on the verb ("slightly",
      "I'd propose", "I would love to") carrying hard content?
- [ ] If it's a redirect, is the spec supplied rather than just the complaint?
- [ ] Does it close with an invitation to push back?
- [ ] Sign-off correct for the language?
- [ ] Bullets instead of any paragraph over three sentences?
- [ ] Russian: diminutive vocative in the greeting? Not over-polished?

## 10. Reference samples

**Directive with a checklist**
> One ask from you - please go through your list and check:
> - the relevance of these items (do they still make sense?)
> - adjust the targets (what needs to be true for next quarter to happen?)
> - add missing / relevant (are there any new ones to add?)

**Russian, short and functional**
> Привет!
> В Июне, с учетом что продолжает работать текущий вариант, мы комфортно можем
> покрыть 30 милионов (можно и больше но не советуется ввиду спада в Июле).
> В Июле, по песимистическому сценарию 21 милион, по оптимистическому 27 милионов.
>
> Эрик

**Latvian, declining news gracefully**
> Sveiks!
> Paldies par feedback. Žēl, bet nu ko darīt... Saprotu. Keep in touch ;)
>
> Ēriks
